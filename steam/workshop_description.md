# Trouble In Armaville

You don't know who the killers are. Neither does anyone else, except them and each other.

Trouble In Armaville brings Trouble In Terrorist Town to Arma 3: the same hidden-role deduction game that's kept GMod players lying to each other for two decades, now played out with real ballistics, real vehicles, and a sandbox big enough to actually hide a body in. Drop a full server into a walled-off town with one shared secret: some of you are Traitors, and nobody innocent knows who.

## The game

Everyone starts a civilian with nothing but their eyes and their instincts. A hidden minority are Traitors, working together, arming up, picking people off quietly enough that nobody connects the dots until it's too late. One publicly known Detective stands between the two sides, armed with actual investigative tools instead of a hunch. And somewhere in the mix, a Jester, whose entire win condition is talking an innocent person into ruining their own day.

Nobody announces a death over the radio. A body has to be found, and even then it takes real investigation to learn who they were and what killed them. That gap between "someone's missing" and "here's what actually happened" is where the game lives: the accusations, the second-guessing, the Traitor sweating through a conversation they really should have stayed out of.

Bring the whole thing to a full Arma server and it stops being a guessing game about hitboxes and becomes one about people. Who hesitated. Who wasn't where they said they'd be. Who suddenly owns a silenced pistol they can't quite explain.

## Play it your way

Trouble In Armaville doesn't care what modpack you're running. Load whatever you already play with and the shops, loot, and airdrops build themselves around it, no compatibility patch, no separate gear pack to track down. Plain vanilla Arma 3 works fine too, if that's more your speed.

For the full experience, we'd still point you at CBA_A3 and ACE3 for the extra depth, plus a radio mod of your choice (ACRE2 or TFAR both work) for real proximity voice. Nothing sells "I definitely didn't just kill our teammate" quite like having to lie about it out loud, within earshot, while the body's still warm.

## Look the part

Not everyone wants the same HUD, so nobody's stuck with one. Cycle through several role crest styles on the fly, including a colorblind-safe palette designed in from the start rather than bolted on afterward. Pick whatever actually reads clearly for your eyes and get back to the game, instead of squinting at a color you can't tell apart from three others while somebody sneaks up behind you.

## Why it holds up round after round

A fresh arena every round, on any of five stock terrains, so no two games play out on the same ground twice. Credits earned for playing your role well, spent on gear that shifts the investigation as much as the firefight. Karma that remembers how you played last round, whether you like it or not. And a genuine reason to keep believing the person next to you, right up until you don't.

New to TTT? A first-timer's guide sits right in the map screen, and every round opens with a quiet, private reminder of exactly what your role is and who you're allowed to trust. No rulebook to memorize before your first round, it's already there the moment you need it.

## Before you go live

Turn off Kill Messages in your server's difficulty settings. Skip it and Arma will happily announce every kill to the whole server the moment it happens, which turns a murder mystery into a very short, very boring press release. Details on the [GitHub page](https://github.com/AdamWaldie/TTTARMA3).
